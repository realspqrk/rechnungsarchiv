VERDICT changes: 10

1. **P0 — Stored evidence does not make one TSA bulletproof (ADR-0004).** After stealing a TSU key, an attacker can forge an earlier genTime and reuse historical good OCSP/CRL material; neither that material nor TL history proves when this particular token existed. RFC 3161 §4 explicitly distrusts tokens under a compromised key. **Minimal fix:** remove the unconditional bankruptcy/offline guarantee; process later compromise notices and revocation reasons, never accept merely because genTime precedes revocation. Recovery requires independently authenticated pre-compromise evidence, such as an authentic issuance log. Otherwise report untrusted/indeterminate. One TSA remains acceptable with this disclosed limitation; backups cannot cure it. [RFC 3161](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161.html#section-4).

2. **P0 — Qualification cannot be inferred from a provider chain plus OID (ADR-0004).** A shared CA may issue qualified and nonqualified services; a stale pin may survive withdrawal. **Minimal fix:** bind the actual signer to the applicable TSA/QTST service digital identity, historical status interval and applicable service qualifiers at genTime. Preserve authenticated national TL/LOTL evidence and its trust bootstrap, not merely extracted certificates. Define refresh/nextUpdate and rollback handling; stale evidence must not establish qualification for newly issued tokens. Expose the trust-data date for offline results. A later withdrawal does not automatically invalidate earlier stamps, nor automatically validate them. [Commission DSS](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/DSS/webapp-demo/doc/dss-documentation.html).

3. **P0 — The normative bag profile is absent (ADR-0002 references missing docs/spec.md).** BagIt alone permits optional tag coverage: omitted chain/retention metadata can change without changing the timestamped root. Duplicate ZIP names can make verifier and extractor see different invoices. **Minimal fix:** specify mandatory authenticated tags and payload manifests, exact-byte hashing, full inventory checks, and rejection of duplicate/colliding paths, traversal, links and ambiguous encodings; bound parsing. Exclude tag manifests from tag manifests. [BagIt](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8493.html).

4. **P1 — “Store revocation status” lacks validation rules (ADR-0004).** An unrelated, stale or unauthorized OCSP response could satisfy an existence-only check. **Minimal fix:** verify signatures, responder authorization, certificate/issuer binding, applicable chain coverage, thisUpdate/nextUpdate and historical freshness; specify equivalent CRL checks. If OCSP fails, use valid CRL evidence; if neither works, retain the token as Unbestimmt and retry collecting evidence. Never substitute a later generic “good” response without adequate historical coverage. [RFC 6960](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6960.html).

5. **P1 — Protocol and clock acceptance remain underspecified (ADR-0004).** Checking for a timestamping EKU alone accepts a nonconforming certificate; a signed erroneous TSA clock can still pass cryptography. **Minimal fix:** require critical, exclusive timestamping EKU, strict CMS/ESS signer binding, permitted algorithms, requested-policy matching and bounded malformed-input rejection. Add malformed-input tests. Use token/policy accuracy for the “spätestens” upper bound; flag implausible time or response delay. Local-clock checks detect accidents, not malicious TSA backdating. [RFC 3161](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161.html).

6. **P1 — Pending data has no externally proven capture date (ADR-0002/0006).** An owner can replace an unstamped bag and roll back the PC clock, suppressing “Nachträglich archiviert”; later stamping authenticates only the replacement. **Minimal fix:** revalidate the entire committed bag before requesting its stamp; identify local capture dates and Belegjahr as assertions. Derive every proven date exclusively from verified timestamp evidence. Pending means no externally established time; a later stamp cannot retroactively close that window.

7. **P1 — Per-file rename is not a complete transaction (ADR-0002/0003).** A crash can leave an orphan ZIP, lost request or index referencing absent evidence; fixed filenames conflict with append-only retries. **Minimal fix:** define durable commit/recovery order, hash the bytes actually copied, fsync before publishing, use no-replace commits and immutable attempt filenames. Treat index.json as rebuildable or version it. Recover pending deposits without overwriting committed evidence.

8. **P1 — A .lock is not cross-PC coordination (ADR-0003).** Synced replicas can each acquire their own lock and create independently valid forks. **Minimal fix:** support one local writer; reject unsupported shared/synced writing. Verify archive ID, exact predecessor root, sequence increment and genesis. Hidden competing branches remain undetectable; qualify completeness wording without restoring external checkpoints.

9. **P1 — UI overstates proof.** Einstellungen says “Richtigkeit von Zeit und Inhalt”; Pruefen claims files were never changed, although replacement followed by restoration is invisible. **Minimal fix:** use “Richtigkeit der Zeitangabe und Unversehrtheit der Daten” and “stimmen mit dem spätestens am … belegten Stand überein”; show qualification per deposit, including Unbestimmt. [eIDAS Article 41](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910).

10. **P2 — Cut false longevity hedges.** SHA-512 behind a SHA-256 root provides no independent protection against failure of that root commitment; byte-deterministic ZIPs do not strengthen a bag-root proof. **Minimal fix:** drop those requirements unless interoperability needs them. Retain an explicit migration-before-cryptographic-weakness responsibility; backups alone cannot guarantee 22-year verifiability. Renewal may remain deferred, but unconditional longevity claims cannot. [RFC 4998](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4998.html).
